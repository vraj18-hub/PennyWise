What is financial planning?
 
Financial planning means deciding what you want your money to do for you, such as buying a home, funding education, or retiring comfortably, and then organizing your income, savings, insurance, and investments to get there. A good plan is written down, reviewed at least once a year, and adjusted when your life changes.
 
In what order should I handle my money?
 
A widely used order of priorities is as follows. First, cover essential expenses and make sure you are not spending more than you earn. Second, build an emergency fund of three to six months of expenses. Third, get adequate term insurance if you have dependents and health insurance for yourself and your family. Fourth, pay off high-interest debt such as credit card balances. Fifth, invest regularly for your long-term goals, and take advantage of tax-saving options where they fit your goals. Skipping the earlier steps can force you to sell investments at a bad time.
 
What is goal-based investing?
 
Goal-based investing means attaching each investment to a specific goal with a target amount and a date. For example, you might plan for a house down payment in seven years and retirement in thirty. Knowing how much you need and when tells you how much to invest each month and how much risk is sensible for each goal.
 
How does my time horizon affect what I should invest in?
 
The time you have until you need the money matters a lot. For goals less than about three years away, prefer savings accounts, fixed deposits, or debt funds, because shares can fall right before you need the cash. For goals three to five years away, a mix of debt and equity may suit you. For goals more than five to seven years away, equity funds are commonly used because they have more time to recover from downturns. As a goal gets closer, gradually move money from equity toward safer options.
 
What is risk tolerance?
 
Risk tolerance is how much fluctuation in the value of your investments you can accept, both financially and emotionally. Someone with a stable income and a long horizon can usually take more risk than someone close to retirement. If a fall of 20 percent in your portfolio would make you sell everything in panic, you are better off with a more conservative mix.
 
How much should I save for retirement?
 
A commonly used rough guide is that you need a retirement corpus of about 25 times your expected annual expenses at retirement, based on the idea that you can withdraw around 4 percent a year. Because prices rise over the decades, your future expenses will be much higher than today's, so it is important to account for inflation. The earlier you start, the smaller the monthly amount you need, because compounding does more of the work. Common retirement options in India include the Employees' Provident Fund, the Public Provident Fund, the National Pension System, and equity mutual funds.
 
What is a step-up SIP?
 
A step-up SIP is a systematic investment plan where the monthly amount increases automatically by a chosen percentage every year, for example when your salary rises. It helps your investments keep pace with your income and can significantly increase the final amount compared with a flat SIP.
 
What is rebalancing?
 
Rebalancing means adjusting your portfolio back to your target mix. For example, if your plan is 60 percent equity and 40 percent debt, but a strong market pushes equity to 75 percent, you would sell some equity or add to debt to return to the plan. Doing this about once a year keeps your risk level in line with your goals.
 
What are common investing mistakes to avoid?
 
Common mistakes include investing without an emergency fund, trying to time the market, chasing last year's best-performing fund, stopping SIPs when markets fall, putting all money into one stock or sector, mixing insurance with investment, and taking investment tips from social media or friends without checking them. Consistency, diversification, and patience usually beat cleverness.
 
How can I spot financial scams and fraud?
 
Be suspicious of guaranteed high returns, pressure to invest quickly, promises of doubling your money, tips shared in messaging groups, and anyone asking for your OTP, PIN, or password. Your bank or a government agency will never ask for these. Before trusting an adviser or platform, check that they are registered with SEBI, and for insurance check that the insurer is registered with IRDAI. If an offer sounds too good to be true, it almost certainly is, and you can report fraud to the national cybercrime helpline.
