What is a budget?
 
A budget is a plan for how you will spend and save your income each month. It helps you see where your money goes and makes sure your priorities, such as rent, bills, and savings, are covered before you spend on things you can do without. A budget is not about restricting yourself, it is about choosing on purpose.
 
What is the 50/30/20 rule?
 
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting guideline. About 50 percent of your after-tax income goes to needs such as rent, groceries, utilities, transport, insurance premiums, and loan EMIs. About 30 percent goes to wants such as eating out, entertainment, and shopping. About 20 percent goes to savings and investing. For example, on a monthly income of 50,000 rupees, that means about 25,000 for needs, 15,000 for wants, and 10,000 for savings and investments. It is a starting point, and you can adjust the percentages to your situation, for example when rent in your city takes more than half your income.
 
What is a savings rate?
 
Your savings rate is the percentage of your income that you keep instead of spending. It is calculated as income minus expenses, divided by income, multiplied by 100. For example, if you earn 40,000 rupees and spend 30,000, you save 10,000, which is a savings rate of 25 percent. Many planners suggest aiming for at least 20 percent, and a higher rate helps you reach goals faster. A savings rate that is negative means you are spending more than you earn and are likely relying on debt.
 
What is an emergency fund and how big should it be?
 
An emergency fund is money set aside only for unexpected costs, such as losing your job, a medical bill, or an urgent repair. A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses, and closer to six months or more if your income is irregular or you have dependents. For example, if your essential monthly expenses are 30,000 rupees, a target emergency fund would be between 90,000 and 1,80,000 rupees. Build it before you start investing in anything risky.
 
Where should I keep my emergency fund?
 
An emergency fund should be safe and easy to access quickly. Good places are a savings account, a liquid mutual fund, or a short fixed deposit that can be broken without a large penalty. Do not keep it in shares or anything whose value could drop just when you need the money.
 
What does pay yourself first mean?
 
Paying yourself first means moving your savings and investments out of your account as soon as you receive your income, instead of saving whatever is left at the end of the month. The simplest way is to automate it with a standing instruction or a SIP that runs on the day after your salary arrives. This works better than relying on willpower.
 
How do I find out where I am overspending?
 
Track your spending for at least a month and group it into categories such as food, transport, rent, entertainment, and shopping. Compare each category with your income and with guidelines like the 50/30/20 rule. Categories that take a large and growing share, especially discretionary ones like dining out and subscriptions, are the best candidates to trim. Small recurring costs such as unused subscriptions add up to a large amount over a year.
 
What is lifestyle inflation?
 
Lifestyle inflation is when your spending rises every time your income rises, so you never end up saving more. To avoid it, decide in advance that a fixed share of every raise goes to savings and investments before you adjust your lifestyle.
 
How should I pay off debt?
 
Two popular methods are the avalanche method and the snowball method. With the avalanche method, you pay the minimum on all debts and put every extra rupee toward the debt with the highest interest rate, which saves the most money. With the snowball method, you focus on the smallest balance first for quick wins and motivation. Whichever you choose, clear high-interest debt such as credit card balances and personal loans before investing, because the interest you pay is usually higher than the returns you can reliably earn.
 
What is a sinking fund?
 
A sinking fund is money you set aside gradually for a known future expense, such as an annual insurance premium, a vacation, a festival, or a gadget. Saving a little each month means a large bill does not disrupt your budget or push you toward a loan.
 
How much can I safely invest every month?
 
Start with your monthly income and subtract essential expenses and any EMIs. Then set aside money for your emergency fund until it is complete, and keep a small buffer for irregular costs. What remains is your investable surplus. As a rough guide, many people aim to invest 15 to 20 percent of income, and it is better to start with an amount you can keep up consistently and raise it as your income grows than to start too high and stop.
