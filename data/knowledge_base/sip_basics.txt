What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan, or SIP, is a method of investing a fixed amount of money into a mutual fund at regular intervals, typically every month. Instead of investing a large lump sum at once, the investor commits to smaller, regular contributions.

How Compounding Works in a SIP

Each contribution buys units of the mutual fund at the prevailing price. Over time, the returns generated by earlier investments are reinvested along with new contributions, which means future growth is calculated on a progressively larger base. This effect is called compounding, and it becomes more powerful the longer the SIP continues.

Equity vs Debt Mutual Funds

Equity mutual funds primarily invest in shares of companies and tend to offer higher potential returns with higher risk and volatility. Debt mutual funds invest in fixed-income instruments such as bonds and government securities, offering more stable but generally lower returns. Many investors combine both types depending on their risk tolerance and time horizon.

Old vs New Income Tax Regime in India

The old tax regime allows taxpayers to claim various deductions and exemptions, such as under Section 80C for investments, but applies higher tax rates. The new tax regime offers lower tax rates but removes most deductions and exemptions. Individuals should compare their total taxable income and eligible deductions under both regimes to determine which results in a lower tax liability for their situation.
