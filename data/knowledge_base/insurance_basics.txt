Why do I need insurance?
 
Insurance protects you and your family from large, unexpected financial losses, such as a medical emergency, an accident, or the death of the person who earns for the household. You pay a small regular amount called a premium, and in return the insurer pays a large sum if a covered event happens. Insurance is meant for protection, not for building wealth.
 
What is term insurance?
 
Term insurance is a pure life cover. You pay a premium for a fixed period, and if you die during that period, your nominee receives the sum assured. If you survive the term, nothing is paid back. Because it offers only protection, its premiums are much lower than other life insurance products. It is best for anyone with dependents, such as a spouse, children, or parents, or with loans that the family would struggle to repay.
 
How much term insurance cover do I need?
 
A common rule of thumb is a cover of about 10 to 15 times your annual income, adjusted upward if you have large loans or many dependents. The aim is that your family could repay debts and cover living costs and major goals such as education without your income. Buying early and while healthy keeps premiums lower, because premiums are fixed based on your age and health when you buy.
 
What is the difference between term, endowment, and ULIP plans?
 
Term plans give only life cover at a low cost. Endowment and money-back plans combine insurance with savings, but they usually give low returns and need a much higher premium for the same cover. A unit linked insurance plan, or ULIP, mixes insurance with market-linked investing and has charges that can be high in the early years. Many financial planners suggest keeping insurance and investing separate, by buying term insurance for protection and investing the rest in mutual funds or other products.
 
What is health insurance?
 
Health insurance pays for hospital treatment costs up to the sum insured, either by reimbursing you or by settling the bill directly with the hospital in a cashless claim. A single medical emergency can cost lakhs of rupees, so health insurance protects your savings. Policies can be individual, covering one person, or family floater, where one sum insured is shared across the family.
 
What should I check before buying a health insurance policy?
 
Look at the sum insured, and whether it is enough for hospital costs in your city. Check the waiting period for pre-existing conditions, which is often a few years, and any sub-limits such as a cap on room rent. See whether there is co-payment, where you pay a percentage of every claim. Check the list of network hospitals for cashless treatment, the exclusions, and whether the policy offers a no-claim bonus that increases the cover for each claim-free year. Buying early keeps premiums lower and waiting periods behind you.
 
What is a claim settlement ratio?
 
The claim settlement ratio shows the percentage of claims an insurer paid out compared with the claims it received in a year. A higher ratio suggests the insurer is more reliable in paying claims, although you should also check how quickly it settles them and how many claims it rejects. Reading the policy document carefully matters as much as the ratio.
 
What is motor insurance?
 
In India, at least third-party motor insurance is mandatory for every vehicle on the road. Third-party cover pays for damage or injury you cause to other people. Comprehensive cover adds protection for damage to your own vehicle, such as accidents, theft, and natural disasters.
 
What is a premium, sum assured, and nominee?
 
The premium is the amount you pay to keep the policy active, and missing payments can make the policy lapse. The sum assured or sum insured is the maximum amount the insurer will pay. The nominee is the person who receives the money if you die, so keep the nominee details up to date and tell your family about your policies.
 
Who regulates insurance in India?
 
Insurance companies in India are regulated by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India, or IRDAI. Buy policies only from IRDAI-registered insurers, be honest about your health and habits when applying, because hiding facts can lead to a rejected claim later.
